High availability and load balancing on AWS

High availability means your application keeps working when something breaks. Here you can experiment with Availability Zones, load balancers and Auto Scaling: move the sliders, break things on purpose and watch what happens. A soft voice explains each change. Press Play story for a guided tour of any tab.

Load balancerLayer and protocolsRemember for the exam
ALB ApplicationLayer 7: HTTP, HTTPS, gRPC, WebSocketRoutes by path, host, header or query string. Targets: instances, IPs, Lambda. Redirects, fixed responses, Cognito or OIDC login. No static IP. Cross-zone is always on.
NLB NetworkLayer 4: TCP, UDP, TLSExtreme performance and ultra-low latency. Static IP or Elastic IP per AZ. Keeps the client IP. TLS passthrough. Works with PrivateLink. Cross-zone is off by default.
GWLB GatewayLayer 3 (GENEVE)Sends traffic through third-party virtual appliances such as firewalls.
CLB ClassicLayer 4 and 7, legacyOld generation. New designs use ALB or NLB.
Auto Scaling conceptRemember for the exam
Min, desired, maxThe group keeps the desired number of instances, between min and max. It spreads them across the AZs you choose.
Health checksEC2 checks are the default. Turn on ELB health checks so the group also replaces instances that fail the load balancer’s check. Health check grace period default: 300 seconds.
Scaling policiesTarget tracking (simplest, such as keep CPU near 50%), step scaling, simple scaling, scheduled scaling, predictive scaling.
Scale on requestsALBRequestCountPerTarget is a ready-made metric for target tracking.
Deregistration delayConnection draining for target groups. Default 300 seconds, so in-flight requests finish before an instance is removed.

Teaching simulations: the numbers are small and made up so the effects are easy to see. Quotas and defaults change, so check AWS’s current documentation before relying on an exact number. Voice uses your browser’s built-in speech, and sound starts after your first click.
